How to play

  1. Sign in with a name. Each guess is saved as you go, so you can close the tab and sign in with the same name to continue.
  2. Each plot is a letter drawn in 1,000 dots. Estimate the correlation r between x and y, from −1 to +1, with the slider or by typing a number.
  3. Press Enter to lock in your guess. You then see the true r and the best-fit line. Locked guesses can't be changed.
  4. Answer all 26 letters, in any order, to join the leaderboard. It ranks players by mean squared error: the average of your squared misses, so lower is better and big misses cost the most.

What correlation means

Correlation measures how closely the points follow a straight line. Pearson's r runs from −1 to +1. At +1 every point sits on a rising line, at −1 on a falling line, and near 0 there is no straight-line trend at all.

The steepness of the line doesn't matter, only how tightly the points hug it. A shape can also be very orderly and still have r near 0: the letter O is a ring, and its rising and falling halves cancel out.

The formula

r= Σ (xi − x)(yi − y) √Σ (xi − x)² · Σ (yi − y)²

x and y are the averages of the x and y values, and each sum runs over every point. In words: r is the covariance of x and y divided by the product of their standard deviations. The top is positive when points above average in x tend to be above average in y, and the bottom scales the result to sit between −1 and +1.
